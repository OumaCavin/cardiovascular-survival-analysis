## 1. Definition of Health Informatics

Health Informatics is the interdisciplinary field that combines healthcare, information science, computer science, and management to optimize the collection, storage, retrieval, analysis, and dissemination of health information. It involves the use of information technology, data analytics, and computational methods to improve healthcare delivery, patient outcomes, and health system efficiency.

Health informatics encompasses the design, development, adoption, and application of information technology-based innovations in healthcare services, including electronic health records (EHR), clinical decision support systems, health information exchanges, and telemedicine platforms.

## 7. Challenges of Health Informatics

### 7.1 Technical Challenges
- **Interoperability** - Different systems failing to communicate
- **Data Quality** - Incomplete, inconsistent, or inaccurate data
- **Data Security** - Protecting sensitive health information
- **System Integration** - Connecting legacy systems with new technologies

### 7.2 Organizational Challenges
- **Resistance to Change** - Staff reluctance to adopt new systems
- **Training** - Adequate staff education and skills development
- **Workflow Disruption** - Temporary productivity losses during transition
- **Resource Constraints** - Limited budgets and IT infrastructure

### 7.3 Regulatory Challenges
- **Privacy Compliance** - HIPAA, GDPR, and other regulations
- **Data Governance** - Ownership, access, and usage policies
- **Standards Adoption** - Implementing industry data standards
- **Legal Liability** - Responsibility for errors and breaches

### 7.4 Data Challenges
- **Data Volume** - Managing massive amounts of health data
- **Data Variety** - Structured, unstructured, and semi-structured data
- **Data Velocity** - Real-time data processing requirements
- **Data Veracity** - Ensuring data accuracy and reliability

## 8. Types of Health Systems

### 8.1 Based on Ownership

**Public Health Systems**
- Government-funded and operated
- Focus on population health
- Universal access provisions
- Examples: NHS (UK), Medicare (Australia)

**Private Health Systems**
- Privately funded and operated
- Market-driven services
- Insurance-based access
- Examples: Private hospitals in the US

**Mixed Health Systems**
- Combination of public and private
- Dual-tier service delivery
- Government regulation with private participation

### 8.2 Based on Delivery Model

**Centralized Systems**
- Single national database
- Top-down governance
- Standardized procedures
- Examples: Scandinavian countries

**Decentralized Systems**
- Regional autonomy
- Distributed databases
- Local decision-making
- Examples: United States

### 8.3 Based on Financing

**Beveridge Model**
- Tax-funded healthcare
- Single-payer system
- Government ownership of facilities

**Bismarck Model**
- Social health insurance
- Multiple insurers
- Private providers

**Out-of-Pocket Model**
- Direct payment for services
- Limited accessibility
- Market-driven

## 9. Weibull Distribution Calculations

The Weibull distribution is commonly used in survival analysis to model time-to-event data.

### 9.1 Continuous Weibull Distribution

For a Weibull distribution with shape parameter k > 0 and scale parameter λ > 0:

**Probability Density Function (PDF):**
```
f(t) = (k/λ) × (t/λ)^(k-1) × exp(-(t/λ)^k)
```

Where:
- t ≥ 0 (time)
- k = shape parameter (determines hazard function shape)
- λ = scale parameter (characteristic lifetime)

**Survival Function S(t):**
```
S(t) = P(T > t) = exp(-(t/λ)^k)
```

This gives the probability of surviving beyond time t.

**Hazard Function h(t):**
```
h(t) = f(t) / S(t) = (k/λ) × (t/λ)^(k-1)
```

This represents the instantaneous failure rate at time t.

**Cumulative Hazard Function H(t):**
```
H(t) = -log(S(t)) = (t/λ)^k
```

This accumulates hazard over time.

### 9.2 Example Calculation

Given: k = 2 (shape), λ = 10 (scale)

**At time t = 5:**

1. **Survival Function:**
```
S(5) = exp(-(5/10)^2) = exp(-0.25) = 0.779
```
Probability of surviving beyond 5 time units = 77.9%

2. **Hazard Function:**
```
h(5) = (2/10) × (5/10)^(2-1) = 0.2 × 0.5 = 0.1
```
Instantaneous hazard at t = 5

3. **Cumulative Hazard:**
```
H(5) = (5/10)^2 = 0.25
```
Accumulated hazard by time t = 5

### 9.3 Discrete Weibull Distribution (Life Table Format)

For discrete time intervals, Weibull can be approximated:

| Time (t) | n_at_risk | d_event | h(t) | S(t) | H(t) |
|-----------|-----------|---------|------|------|------|
| 0 | 100 | - | - | 1.000 | 0.000 |
| 1 | 100 | 5 | 0.050 | 0.950 | 0.051 |
| 2 | 95 | 7 | 0.074 | 0.879 | 0.129 |
| 3 | 88 | 8 | 0.091 | 0.799 | 0.224 |
| 4 | 80 | 6 | 0.075 | 0.739 | 0.302 |
| 5 | 74 | 5 | 0.068 | 0.689 | 0.372 |

Where:
- n_at_risk = number of subjects at risk at start of interval
- d_event = number of events during interval
- h(t) = d_event / n_at_risk (discrete hazard)
- S(t) = cumulative survival probability
- H(t) = cumulative hazard
